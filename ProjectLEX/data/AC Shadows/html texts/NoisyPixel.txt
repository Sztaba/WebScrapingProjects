Assassin’s Creed Shadows Review – A Stunning Evolution In Open-World Gaming

Assassin’s Creed Shadows Review – A Marvel of Immersive Action & Storytelling

I’ve been doing my best to keep up with Assassin’s Creed Shadows news, Ubisoft’s newest entry in this long-running stealth action series. I’ve read all your criticisms and feedback about the game. I’ve seen the drama and nerd rage, and I took it upon myself to pick up this game for review and judge it based on all the feedback I’ve seen from various anime avatars who paid attention in history class. So whether Yasuke is a samurai or not, whether watermelons are being sold during the wrong season, or maybe even if some Japanese text isn’t accurate, I’m not sure.
However, I can tell you that every bone in my body wanted to take that feedback seriously. But while playing—and I’m being honest—I didn’t care. From a game development standpoint, Assassin’s Creed Shadows is a masterpiece, and that’s evident in the first hour of gameplay. Now, from a narrative perspective, the experience is even better, highlighting the most well-paced open-world active adventure game released in this modern era. What’s more impressive is how unique this game feels when compared to other entries in the series. If this is the future of open-world games, Assassin’s Creed Shadows has set an exceptionally high bar.
Performance and Gameplay Experience

To get the general pre-amble out of the way, I played on PC with a GeForce RTX 4080, and everything ran beautifully. I didn’t encounter any game-breaking bugs during my playthrough—just a few oddities here and there, but nothing that pulled me out of the experience. Sure, enemy AI can be cheesed like in any other stealth action game, but the developers added elements like creaky floors and multiple stealth routes. This slowed me down enough when I wanted to remain undetected, so I rarely saw enemies acting haphazardly. I can’t speak for console performance, but if it’s anything like the PC version, it’ll be solid. For audio, I chose the immersive mode, which has Japanese voice acting, and I can’t recommend it enough. It amps up the atmosphere so much that I honestly think anyone who’s even remotely curious should try it out—trust me, it’s worth it.
A Tale of Vengeance and Growth
Assassin’s Creed Shadows is a story of vengeance that evolves far past those themes. The opening highlights Yasuke’s first encounter with Oda Nobunaga before the story shifts to a young shinobi, Naoe. What’s interesting about this change in perspective is how the writing takes its time to establish Naoe’s characterization. It’ll be hours before you assume the role of Yasuke again, but his presence in Naoe’s personal quest seems to find them unknowingly crossing paths. The pacing in the first 10 hours of this game is immaculate. I felt fully present and had a clear understanding of the situation. Sure, this is an action game, but the bloodlust is first controlled and baited out as we witness Naoe’s upbringing through flashbacks and learn how to gain a seat at various tables using political tactics that must first be understood.
Knowledge and Exploration: Core Gameplay Mechanics
This theme of knowledge and invisible threads of information is commonplace throughout the experience. Players will add scouts to their growing community, which are needed to follow clues that lead to objectives. Further, saving citizens can lead to increasing your net of rumors that highlight secret points on your map for resources. This also bleeds into the various collectibles within villages that players must interact with to gain Knowledge points that unlock access to new skills. It all works seamlessly together and makes sense within the concept of the game. Even the scouting system, one you’d expect to grow tiresome when you’re just trying to find your target, became a highlight of the experience as it matched the ever-growing rise of influence that Naoe has over her hidden clan.
Yasuke and Naoe: A Dynamic Duo

Yasuke’s impact on the story is significant. His presence is never wholly accepted at first, but the commonalities and sins he and Naoe share bind them to a familiar path. It’s an incredibly impactful experience as you witness their relationship grow, all while they tackle their personal struggles and come to terms with the brutal reality that comes to light. In the context of history, I did my best to look up characters and events and was wildly surprised by how even Naoe’s presence made sense in these moments. I applaud Ubisoft’s writing team for portraying a convincing timeline that fits into the Assassin’s Creed universe. They really lean into connecting the series further with the Animus hub and key plot points that progress the core story.
A Rich and Rewarding Narrative
I rolled credits at 56 hours, and there’s still plenty left for me to return to and explore. From a narrative standpoint, the story takes its time with its supporting cast in ways that many others overshadow. They give purpose to even simple quest-givers, some of which lead to new allies and access to assistance in battle. Characters like Yaya are even swayed by responses in dialogue, giving each player some agency over the narrative. That said, there are options to turn this off and play through a canonical version of the game. However you play, and whatever you spend your time doing, is consistently rewarded with new story elements that are constantly being tracked in the Objective menu, which ties all of your significant targets and questlines together in an easy-to-follow way. It’s incredibly digestible to plan out your approach to the game, whether you want to mainline the story or spend time clearing milestones and side objectives; it all feels natural while playing. By the end of the game, you can see every point of the journey and every significant chapter to remind you of the entire adventure.
Challenging Combat and Skill Progression
I will say that those who wish to push forward are in for some challenge. The regions divide the game’s difficulty level, but you aren’t really kept from going wherever you want once the world opens up. That said, higher-level enemies will easily take out your characters in a single hit, but if you’re good, you can probably chip away at their health while also evading various attacks. What I’m saying is that this combat system is best experienced by those who take time to understand the pacing and timing while building up their knowledge of enemy telegraphs. I’m not telling you how to play; this is just coming from someone who was humbled by these encounters time and time again. Still, there are multiple difficulty levels to choose from, so you can make it easier or more challenging for your skill level. Playing on Normal difficulty provided a balanced experience, and I never felt like I was too overpowered.
Character-Specific Gameplay and Abilities

Your approach to gameplay will vary depending on whether you’re playing as Naoe or Yasuke. When I was first able to control Yasuke, I had to readjust my approach to combat because he isn’t the stealth assassin that Naoe is and doesn’t have access to her nimble abilities. Still, he comes at a time in the game when I felt myself wanting to be a bit more powerful, and the experience was only elevated by his absolute power. Each of these characters plays drastically differently, which is a shame because they aren’t working together more seamlessly in fights. There are ways to take down enemies cooperatively, but going to a menu and switching manually gets tiresome, even though the loading screen is brief. Utilizing other allies is interesting because they basically act as summons that can distract or clear out enemies.
Skill Trees and Progression Systems
While you obtain Skill points as you level up, some abilities or tools are tied to story progression and knowledge. Gaining knowledge points paces your advancement within the skill tree, forcing you to explore environments as much as you spend fighting. It’s an outstanding balance and a relief because it manages exhaustion over long play sessions. Even elements of the game, like the series’ staple towers, come as enemy bases or environmental milestones. There’s plenty of variety in the game’s side quests, so I can’t really complain about the more annoying systems, such as the meditation mini-game that goes on a little too long.
Intuitive Menus and Accessibility
When it comes to menus, I really appreciate how clean and intuitive everything feels. Navigating the skill trees and inventory is straightforward, and each screen clearly shows what resources you need for upgrades. On top of that, there’s a robust accessibility menu with tons of options. I didn’t get a chance to test them all, but if you’re curious about text sizes, button remapping, or visual filters, you’ll likely find something that helps tailor the experience to your needs.
Gear and Customization
There is gear you can purchase with rare in-game currency. It’s not essential, but it looks cool, and you can definitely show off your stealth flair if you collect enough of that currency around the world. It doesn’t feel intrusive, and I never got the sense that the best equipment was locked behind a paywall or anything shady. From a broader standpoint, Shadows shares some DNA with past entries like Assassin’s Creed Origins or Odyssey, especially in its open-world approach and tiered enemies. Yet it shifts the focus more heavily on stealth and narrative pacing, leaning on a historical timeline that feels meticulous in detail—much like those earlier games did in their respective settings.
Combat Depth and Stealth Mechanics

There are plenty of videos out there that break down the combat of this game, so I’d hate to explain every nuance if only to keep this review as brief as possible. On the surface level, going prone changes everything. Every system in the game lends itself to a creative means of enemy takedowns. In the opening hours, getting caught is a bit more detrimental, but as you learn new abilities, you gain access to distractions and smoke bombs—or you can just switch to Yasuke and tank your way through battle. I never felt invincible, and resources aren’t exceptionally easy to come across during battles. Before entering any fight, some scouting is needed. There’s an eagle eye mode that allows you to see enemies and treasure, along with plenty of possible infiltration techniques. You’re free to approach these bases as you wish, but the bigger castles require you to defeat the Samurai guards to unlock the most valuable loot. This is something you can return to, but I could never resist trying to take on these powerful enemies whenever I saw them.
Base Building and Resource Management
I also appreciated how collectibles and resources aren’t completely littered across every town. Instead, they’re more confined and used to build your base. There are even ways to utilize limited scouts to smuggle large quantities of items at the end of each season. Building the base becomes another arm of the experience, with progression tied to the creation or upgrading of specific buildings. It’s approachable without being overly complex, and if you want a truly zen hideout, you can spend hours perfecting every detail. Plus, weapon crafting and upgrading add unique buffs to your gear. The ease of navigating all these systems can’t be overstated; I was never lost or confused about what I wanted to do next.
A Stunning Open World
Now, let me talk about the best part of this entire experience: the environment. Assassin’s Creed Shadows is a gorgeous game. I don’t know how this team pulled off the scale and lush plant life of this era, but I was widely impressed. The open world isn’t compact by any means, yet the natural flow of roads and pathways leads you to interesting points without feeling forced. Veering off the beaten path almost always rewards you with a unique quest or collectible, fueling that sense of discovery. Then the seasons shift, and you’re treated to the best weather system of any modern-era game. There’s nothing like emerging from a snowy forest into a vibrant spring village or storming a castle under pouring rain. I actually had to remind myself to close my mouth because the visuals were so jaw-dropping.

Assassin’s Creed Shadows is a marvel of game design and narrative writing that not only elevates this series but pushes the open-world formula forward. It streamlines its many systems, encourages genuine exploration, and paces its narrative so that every milestone feels meaningful. Thanks to a lengthy review embargo, I didn’t have to rush through, which only enhanced my appreciation of the game’s intricate details. I approached this as a casual player and left feeling like a skilled assassin. Whether you’re drawn in by the historical drama, the stealth mechanics, or simply want to roam a sprawling, gorgeous world, Shadows is worth your time. For newcomers and series veterans alike, this entry proves that Assassin’s Creed still has plenty of surprises—and a truly high standard for whatever comes next.

Assassin’s Creed Shadows is a breathtaking evolution of the open-world formula, blending masterful storytelling, refined stealth mechanics, and stunning visuals. With a gripping dual-protagonist narrative and meticulously crafted historical setting, Ubisoft delivers one of the most immersive Assassin’s Creed experiences to date.
