Assassin’s Creed Shadows PS5 Review: A Worthy Leap of Faith

Codename Red is all we had to go off of for the next main installment in the Assassin’s Creed series. An AC game set in feudal Japan was nothing but a distant dream asked by fans of the franchise but Ubisoft Quebec has finally delivered. After being delayed twice, Assassin’s Creed Shadows is finally seeing the light of day, offering a refreshing departure from the series’ traditional storytelling by focusing on dual protagonists. Usually, being delayed multiple times raises a big red flag over the game’s overall quality and direction, but I’d say the wait for this one was well worth it. 

 

Welcome to Feudal Japan

Thematically, Shadows delves into honor, political conflict, and family legacy, set against the backdrop of Japan’s Azuchi-Momoyama period. The story grounds itself in real events and figures, such as Oda Nobunaga and even Yasuke himself. The narrative follows the intertwined stories of two heroes: Naoe, a fictional shinobi assassin and daughter of Fujibayashi Nagato, a master ninja of the Iga clan, and Yasuke, a historical African samurai who served under the powerful daimyo Nobunaga.

Naoe’s story is deeply personal, as she navigates a world of war and deception while uncovering secrets about her lineage and the mysterious past of her mother. Yasuke, on the other hand, is initially aligned with Nobunaga but becomes involved in Naoe’s quest, leading to a convergence of their stories as they confront the tumultuous political landscape of feudal Japan. And since it’s an Assassin’s Creed game, there’s also a connection to the Brotherhood as well, with Naoe tied to it through her heritage and training. This connection is gradually revealed as she learns more about her family’s past and the broader context of the Assassin Brotherhood.

A Canon Narrative Experience

Compared to earlier games like Odyssey and Valhalla, which leaned heavily into branching narratives with significant player choices, Shadows scales back the impact of such decisions. While choices are still present, they play a less pivotal role in shaping the core narrative. There’s even a “Canon Mode,” which offers a linear narrative experience for those who prefer a no-choices-needed version of the story.

You actually spend the first 10-12 hours of the game, including the prologue, playing only as Naoe (though you do get a brief moment with Yasuke), learning about her backstory, and getting to know her intentions and motivations. It isn’t until a good chunk into the narrative that Naoe and Yasuke’s paths cross, allowing you to play as either of the two main characters. That being said, playing as Yasuke during exploration is highly disadvantageous, as he can’t use a grappling hook to scale buildings and generally moves much slower than Naoe.

Improved Combat Mechanics

I was thoroughly impressed and surprised by the combat offered in Shadows, as it’s vastly more refined compared to Mirage’s mediocre affair. There’s a natural ebb and flow to head-on fights, as the game implements a crisp parry system along with a perfect dodge. The game encourages you to recognize and adapt to enemy patterns, reacting to attacks that must be parried or dodged. Additionally, environmental factors such as weather and time of day can influence combat strategies by affecting enemy behavior and visibility. 

The stealth mechanics have also been enhanced, most notably the dynamic lighting system that allows Naoe to become invisible when hiding in shadows. She can also go prone, allowing her to roll and move stealthily on the ground or underwater. The return of double assassinations and the introduction of a manual grab mechanic further enrich the stealth experience, providing you with more control during gameplay. It is a role-playing game, after all.

 

The Brute Samurai and The Nimble Shinobi

Yasuke, being a brute samurai, excels in direct combat, as he can use his raw strength to tank enemy hits and deal devastating damage. He uses weapons like the kanabo, a heavy club, and the nagata, a longer-range spear-like weapon, to deal significant damage. Yasuke’s combat style is more aggressive and less agile compared to Naoe, focusing on brute force rather than stealth. He lacks traditional assassin abilities, such as eagle vision, and his movements are less fluid, making him less suitable for parkour and stealth maneuvers.

In contrast, Naoe focuses more on agility and precision, targeting enemy weak spots, while she is much more vulnerable in prolonged fights. She uses weapons like the katana, kusarigama, and tanto, often dual-wielding the latter with her hidden blade. Naoe’s combat style emphasizes speed and precision, allowing her to dodge and deflect attacks before striking at key moments. 

 

Shared Progression

Progression comes in the form of Mastery points and Knowledge ranks. Mastery points are earned by leveling up, completing quests, and defeating challenging enemies, while Knowledge points are gained through non-violent activities such as meditation, discovering lost pages in temples, or learning new Kata. Each character has unique Mastery trees tied to their specific archetypes—samurai for Yasuke and shinobi/assassin for Naoe—and the weapons they use. These trees enable you to unlock new combos, increase weapon efficacy, or acquire deadly new abilities. My favorite is Naoe’s active tanto skill that allows her to auto dodge enemy attacks! What’s nice is that both Naoe and Yasuke share their progression, so you can truly feel free to switch between characters at any time with no repercussions. 

Of course, we have to talk about gear, too! Shadows features a robust equipment system, where you can equip headgear, armor, trinkets, and two weapons, each with its own stats and quality levels. There’s even a transmog system that allows you to customize the appearance of your gear without affecting its stats!

Infinite Zen of Activities to Do

Trust me when I say that this game is jam-packed with content. Did you know there’s even a base-building aspect? For those into a more laid-back and cozy experience, this game has got just that for you. You have complete jurisdiction over how you want to build your hideout, using resources gathered like wood, minerals, and crops. The seasonal system also affects resource availability, requiring you to regularly check your hideout for new materials. The base-building mode offers a tile-based system, where you can build and place buildings, paths, and decorative elements like gardens, statues, and lights. Each building has a specific function, such as the Forge for upgrading gear, the Dojo for training allies, and the Study for improving scout capabilities.

This is also not to mention the plethora of side activities to enrich your core gameplay experience. Naoe can participate in “Kuji-kiri” meditation, a timing and memory challenge, and explore “The Hidden Trails,” which involve parkour and grappling hooks in natural environments. Yasuke, on the other hand, can engage in “Kata,” a martial arts sequence challenge, and “Horse Archery,” where he aims and shoots at targets while riding a horse. Both characters can also visit shrines for prayer, collect lost pages of scrolls at temples, and participate in Sumi-e, an activity where you must approach and paint animals in their natural habitat. 

So-so Performance; Incredible Soundtrack

To no one’s surprise, Assassin’s Creed Shadows comes in at a gigantic 102 GB in size on the PlayStation 5. This is a full-fledged open-world AC game, after all. For reference, I believe Valhalla was around 144 GB in size. Load times are serviceable, taking around 13-14 seconds to load in from the main menu. In terms of performance, three modes are offered: Fidelity targeting 30 fps and ray tracing, Balanced targeting 40 fps at an upscaled 4K resolution, and Performance targeting 60 fps. I played solely on Performance mode, and for the most part, it’s stable albeit prone to occasional frame drops and stutters, especially in your hideout.

Visually, the game boasts mostly sharp and clear image quality, but there’s weird lighting issues, especially in dark areas, where it’s almost impossible to see anything on the screen, along with visual glitches due to clipping. The audio, on the other hand, is highly immersive, particularly in Japanese voiceover with English subtitles. The musical score is crafted by multiple composer teams, including The Flight and TEKE::TEKE. The Flight, known for their work on Assassin’s Creed Odyssey, brings a blend of traditional Japanese instruments with modern sensibilities to create a unique sound. TEKE::TEKE, a Japanese psych-rock band, contributes with their experimental approach, incorporating traditional instruments like the flute and trombone alongside guitars and cinematic vocals. Awesome stuff!!!

Conclusion

Personally, I had a lot of fun playing Assassin’s Creed Shadows, but for those looking for something that reinvents the wheel, this game sure doesn’t. You’ll still face that open-world fatigue, chasing map markers and checking things off a list to complete. The repetition does settle in, but I do think there is tons of enjoyment to be had from this experience. The developers are working on a New Game Plus mode along with cooperative play down the line, so that’s something to look forward to! Plus, if you pre-ordered the game, the first DLC expansion will be free! This is a step in the right direction from Ubisoft’s end, and I’m all for it.

The wait was well worth it for Assassin’s Creed Shadows. The fans have been asking for an AC game set in Japan, and Ubisoft has finally delivered, with a dual-protagonist experience that offers crisp combat mechanics, a bombastic musical score, and a beautiful visual presentation. Assassin’s Creed is so back!

Final Verdict: 4.5/5

 

 

Available on: PC, PS5 (reviewed), Xbox Series X|S; Publisher: Ubisoft; Developer: Ubisoft; Players: 1; Released: March 20th, 2025; ESRB: M; MSRP: $69.99

Full disclosure: This review is based on a copy of Assassin’s Creed Shadows provided by the publisher.
